Problem 1
Ch. 5 — Simply Supported Beam: Roller Reaction
A horizontal beam $AB$ of length $6\ \mathrm{m}$ is supported by a pin at $A$ (left end) and a roller at $B$ (right end). A vertical downward force $F = 12\ \mathrm{kN}$ acts at point $C$, located $2\ \mathrm{m}$ to the right of $A$. Neglect the weight and size of the beam.
Simply supported beam with one point load
Determine the vertical reaction $B_y$ at the roller.
Solution
4.00 kN
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagram of beam AB
Step 1Draw the free-body diagram: isolate beam $AB$ from its supports. Replace the pin at $A$ with components $A_x$ (assumed to the right) and $A_y$ (assumed up), replace the roller at $B$ with one vertical force $B_y$ (assumed up), and show the applied force $F = 12\ \mathrm{kN}$ pointing down at $C$.
Step 2List knowns and unknowns: $L = 6\ \mathrm{m}$, $AC = 2\ \mathrm{m}$, $F = 12\ \mathrm{kN}$. Unknown reactions are $A_x$, $A_y$, $B_y$; the problem asks for $B_y$.
Step 3State assumptions: the beam is rigid, its weight and thickness are neglected, and the roller can push only perpendicular to its surface, so its reaction is vertical.
Step 4Set axes and sign convention: take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive.
Step 5Check for angled forces: every force is purely horizontal or purely vertical, so no force needs to be resolved into components.
Step 6Sum forces in x: $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x = 0$$ because $A_x$ is the only horizontal force on the beam.
Step 7Choose the moment point: take moments about $A$ because $A_x$ and $A_y$ both pass through $A$ and drop out. The moment arm of $F$ is $2\ \mathrm{m}$ and the moment arm of $B_y$ is $6\ \mathrm{m}$.
Step 8Sum moments about A: $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad B_y(L) - F(AC) = 0$$ ($B_y$ turns the beam counterclockwise about $A$, $F$ turns it clockwise).
Step 9Rearrange for the unknown: $$B_y = \frac{F\,(AC)}{L}$$
Step 10Substitute numbers with units: $$B_y = \frac{(12\ \mathrm{kN})(2\ \mathrm{m})}{6\ \mathrm{m}}$$
Step 11State the result: $B_y = 4.00\ \mathrm{kN}$, acting upward as assumed (positive answer).
Step 12Sum forces in y as a check: $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y + B_y - F = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 12 - 4.00 = 8.00\ \mathrm{kN}$$
Step 13Check reasonableness: the units are $\mathrm{kN\cdot m / m = kN}$, the two vertical reactions add to the $12\ \mathrm{kN}$ load, and the support nearer the load ($A$) carries the larger share.
Worked Solution
Free-body diagram of beam AB
Given: $L = 6\ \mathrm{m}$, $a = AC = 2\ \mathrm{m}$, $F = 12\ \mathrm{kN}$ downward. Find: $B_y$. Free-body diagram: the pin at $A$ is replaced by $A_x$ and $A_y$; the roller at $B$ is replaced by a single vertical force $B_y$; the applied force $F$ acts at $C$. $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x = 0$$ Moments about $A$ (eliminates $A_x$ and $A_y$): $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad B_y(6\ \mathrm{m}) - (12\ \mathrm{kN})(2\ \mathrm{m}) = 0$$ $$B_y = \frac{(12\ \mathrm{kN})(2\ \mathrm{m})}{6\ \mathrm{m}} = 4.00\ \mathrm{kN}\ \text{(upward)}$$ $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y + B_y - F = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 12 - 4.00 = 8.00\ \mathrm{kN}$$ Check: the support nearer the load ($A$) carries the larger share, and $A_y + B_y = 12\ \mathrm{kN}$.
Problem 2
Ch. 5 — Simply Supported Beam with an Inclined Load
Beam $AB$ is $8\ \mathrm{m}$ long, with a pin at $A$ (left end) and a roller at $B$ (right end). A vertical downward force $F_1 = 10\ \mathrm{kN}$ acts at $C$, $2\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$. A force $F_2 = 15\ \mathrm{kN}$ acts at $D$, $5\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$, directed downward and to the left at $60^{\circ}$ below the horizontal. Neglect the weight and size of the beam.
Simply supported beam with a vertical and an inclined load
Determine the magnitude of the pin reaction at $A$.
Solution
14.5 kN
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagram of beam AB with the inclined load resolved
Step 1Draw the free-body diagram: isolate beam $AB$. Show pin components $A_x$ (assumed right) and $A_y$ (assumed up) at $A$, the roller force $B_y$ (assumed up) at $B$, $F_1 = 10\ \mathrm{kN}$ down at $C$, and $F_2 = 15\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $D$ pointing down and to the left at $60^{\circ}$ below the horizontal.
Step 2List knowns and unknowns: $L = 8\ \mathrm{m}$, $AC = 2\ \mathrm{m}$, $AD = 5\ \mathrm{m}$, $F_1 = 10\ \mathrm{kN}$, $F_2 = 15\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $60^{\circ}$. Unknowns: $A_x$, $A_y$, $B_y$; the answer wanted is $|A| = \sqrt{A_x^2 + A_y^2}$.
Step 3State assumptions: rigid beam, weight and thickness neglected (all forces act on the beam axis), roller reaction vertical.
Step 4Set axes and sign convention: take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive.
Step 5Resolve F2 in x: $$F_{2x} = F_2\cos 60^{\circ} = 15(0.500) = 7.50\ \mathrm{kN}\ \text{(to the left)}$$
Step 6Resolve F2 in y: $$F_{2y} = F_2\sin 60^{\circ} = 15(0.8660) = 12.99\ \mathrm{kN}\ \text{(downward)}$$
Step 7Sum forces in x: $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x - F_{2x} = 0$$ since the pin is the only support that can resist a horizontal force.
Step 8Solve for A_x: $A_x = 7.50\ \mathrm{kN}$, to the right as assumed.
Step 9Sum moments about A: point $A$ is chosen because $A_x$, $A_y$ and $F_{2x}$ all pass through it. Arms: $F_1$ at $2\ \mathrm{m}$, $F_{2y}$ at $5\ \mathrm{m}$, $B_y$ at $8\ \mathrm{m}$. $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad B_y(8) - F_1(2) - F_{2y}(5) = 0$$
Step 10Substitute numbers with units: $$B_y = \frac{(10\ \mathrm{kN})(2\ \mathrm{m}) + (12.99\ \mathrm{kN})(5\ \mathrm{m})}{8\ \mathrm{m}} = \frac{84.95\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}}{8\ \mathrm{m}}$$
Step 11Solve for B_y: $B_y = 10.62\ \mathrm{kN}$, upward.
Step 12Sum forces in y: $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y + B_y - F_1 - F_{2y} = 0$$
Step 13Solve for A_y: $$A_y = 10 + 12.99 - 10.62 = 12.37\ \mathrm{kN}\ \text{(upward)}$$
Step 14Combine the pin components: $$|A| = \sqrt{A_x^2 + A_y^2} = \sqrt{(7.50)^2 + (12.37)^2} = 14.5\ \mathrm{kN}$$
Step 15State the result: the pin reaction at $A$ is $14.5\ \mathrm{kN}$, directed up and to the right at $\tan^{-1}(12.37/7.50) = 58.8^{\circ}$ above the horizontal.
Step 16Check with a second moment equation: $$\sum M_B = -12.37(8) + 10(6) + 12.99(3) = -98.97 + 60.00 + 38.97 \approx 0$$ so the reactions are consistent.
Worked Solution
Free-body diagram of beam AB with the inclined load resolved
Given: $L = 8\ \mathrm{m}$; $F_1 = 10\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $2\ \mathrm{m}$; $F_2 = 15\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $5\ \mathrm{m}$, $60^{\circ}$ below the horizontal, pointing down-left. Find: $|A|$. Components of $F_2$: $$F_{2x} = 15\cos 60^{\circ} = 7.50\ \mathrm{kN}\ (\leftarrow),\qquad F_{2y} = 15\sin 60^{\circ} = 12.99\ \mathrm{kN}\ (\downarrow)$$ $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x - 7.50 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_x = 7.50\ \mathrm{kN}$$ $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad B_y(8) - 10(2) - 12.99(5) = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; B_y = \frac{84.95\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}}{8\ \mathrm{m}} = 10.62\ \mathrm{kN}$$ $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y + 10.62 - 10 - 12.99 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 12.37\ \mathrm{kN}$$ $$|A| = \sqrt{A_x^2 + A_y^2} = \sqrt{(7.50)^2 + (12.37)^2} = 14.5\ \mathrm{kN}$$ directed up and to the right at $\tan^{-1}(12.37/7.50) = 58.8^{\circ}$ above the horizontal. Check: $\sum M_B = -12.37(8) + 10(6) + 12.99(3) = -98.97 + 60.00 + 38.97 \approx 0$.
Problem 3
Ch. 5 — Beam with Finite Depth (Size Not Neglected)
A beam is supported by a rocker at $A$ and a pin at $B$; $A$ and $B$ lie on the same horizontal line, $6\ \mathrm{m}$ apart, with $A$ on the left. The top surface of the beam is $0.3\ \mathrm{m}$ above line $AB$. A force $P = 800\ \mathrm{N}$ is applied to the top surface at $D$, $2\ \mathrm{m}$ horizontally from $A$, directed downward and to the right at $60^{\circ}$ below the horizontal. A vertical downward force $Q = 300\ \mathrm{N}$ is applied to the top surface at $E$, $4\ \mathrm{m}$ horizontally from $A$. Neglect the weight of the beam but NOT its size.
Deep beam with loads applied on its top surface
Determine the magnitude of the pin reaction at $B$.
Solution
603 N
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagram of the deep beam
Step 1Draw the free-body diagram: isolate the beam, keeping its depth. Show the rocker force $A_y$ (up) at $A$, the pin components $B_x$ (assumed right) and $B_y$ (assumed up) at $B$, the force $P = 800\ \mathrm{N}$ at $D$ on the top surface pointing down-right at $60^{\circ}$, and $Q = 300\ \mathrm{N}$ down at $E$.
Step 2List knowns and unknowns: $AB = 6\ \mathrm{m}$, $D$ is $2\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$ and $0.3\ \mathrm{m}$ above line $AB$, $E$ is $4\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$, $P = 800\ \mathrm{N}$, $Q = 300\ \mathrm{N}$. Unknowns: $A_y$, $B_x$, $B_y$; wanted $|B|$.
Step 3State assumptions: rigid beam, weight neglected, rocker reaction vertical. The beam depth is NOT neglected, so the horizontal component of $P$ has a moment arm of $0.3\ \mathrm{m}$ about any point on line $AB$.
Step 4Set axes and sign convention: take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive.
Step 5Resolve P in x: $$P_x = P\cos 60^{\circ} = 800(0.500) = 400\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(to the right)}$$
Step 6Resolve P in y: $$P_y = P\sin 60^{\circ} = 800(0.8660) = 692.8\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(downward)}$$
Step 7Sum forces in x: $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad B_x + P_x = 0$$ because only the pin can supply a horizontal reaction.
Step 8Solve for B_x: $B_x = -400\ \mathrm{N}$; the negative sign means the pin pushes on the beam to the left, opposite to the assumed direction.
Step 9Sum moments about B: point $B$ removes $B_x$ and $B_y$. Arms: $A_y$ at $6\ \mathrm{m}$, $P_y$ at $4\ \mathrm{m}$, $P_x$ at $0.3\ \mathrm{m}$ (vertical arm), $Q$ at $2\ \mathrm{m}$. $$\sum M_B = 0:\quad -A_y(6) + P_y(4) - P_x(0.3) + Q(2) = 0$$
Step 10Substitute numbers with units: $$A_y = \frac{(692.8\ \mathrm{N})(4\ \mathrm{m}) - (400\ \mathrm{N})(0.3\ \mathrm{m}) + (300\ \mathrm{N})(2\ \mathrm{m})}{6\ \mathrm{m}} = \frac{3251.3\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}}{6\ \mathrm{m}}$$
Step 11Solve for A_y: $A_y = 541.9\ \mathrm{N}$, upward.
Step 12Sum forces in y: $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y + B_y - P_y - Q = 0$$
Step 13Solve for B_y: $$B_y = 692.8 + 300 - 541.9 = 450.9\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(upward)}$$
Step 14Combine the pin components: $$|B| = \sqrt{B_x^2 + B_y^2} = \sqrt{(400)^2 + (450.9)^2} = 603\ \mathrm{N}$$
Step 15State the result: the pin reaction at $B$ is $603\ \mathrm{N}$, directed up and to the left at $\tan^{-1}(450.9/400) = 48.4^{\circ}$ above the horizontal.
Step 16Check reasonableness: if the depth were ignored, $A_y$ would be $3371.3/6 = 561.9\ \mathrm{N}$; including the $0.3\ \mathrm{m}$ arm lowers it by $120/6 = 20\ \mathrm{N}$, a small but real correction. Units: $\mathrm{N\cdot m/m = N}$.
Worked Solution
Free-body diagram of the deep beam
Given: $AB = 6\ \mathrm{m}$; $P = 800\ \mathrm{N}$ at $D$ ($2\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$, $0.3\ \mathrm{m}$ above line $AB$), $60^{\circ}$ below horizontal, down-right; $Q = 300\ \mathrm{N}$ down at $E$ ($4\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$). Find: $|B|$. Components of $P$: $$P_x = 800\cos 60^{\circ} = 400\ \mathrm{N}\ (\rightarrow),\qquad P_y = 800\sin 60^{\circ} = 692.8\ \mathrm{N}\ (\downarrow)$$ $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad B_x + 400 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; B_x = -400\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(acts to the left)}$$ Moments about $B$. Because the beam has depth, $P_x$ acts $0.3\ \mathrm{m}$ above $B$ and produces a clockwise moment: $$\sum M_B = 0:\quad -A_y(6) + 692.8(4) - 400(0.3) + 300(2) = 0$$ $$A_y = \frac{2771.3 - 120 + 600}{6} = \frac{3251.3\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}}{6\ \mathrm{m}} = 541.9\ \mathrm{N}$$ $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y + B_y - 692.8 - 300 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; B_y = 992.8 - 541.9 = 450.9\ \mathrm{N}$$ $$|B| = \sqrt{(400)^2 + (450.9)^2} = 603\ \mathrm{N}$$ directed up and to the left at $\tan^{-1}(450.9/400) = 48.4^{\circ}$ above the horizontal.
Problem 4
Ch. 5 — Cantilever Beam: Fixed-Support Moment
A cantilevered beam $AB$ of length $2.5\ \mathrm{m}$ is built into a wall (fixed support) at its left end $A$ and is free at its right end $B$. A vertical downward force $F = 600\ \mathrm{N}$ acts at $B$. Neglect the weight of the beam.
Cantilever beam with a load at its free end
Determine the reaction moment $M_A$ at the fixed support.
Solution
1.50 kN·m
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagram of the cantilever beam
Step 1Draw the free-body diagram: isolate beam $AB$ from the wall. A fixed support prevents translation and rotation, so show $A_x$ (assumed right), $A_y$ (assumed up) and a reaction moment $M_A$ (assumed counterclockwise) at $A$, plus $F = 600\ \mathrm{N}$ down at $B$.
Step 2List knowns and unknowns: $L = 2.5\ \mathrm{m}$, $F = 600\ \mathrm{N}$. Unknowns: $A_x$, $A_y$, $M_A$; wanted $M_A$.
Step 3State assumptions: rigid beam, weight neglected, the load acts exactly at the free end.
Step 4Set axes and sign convention: take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive.
Step 5Check for angled forces: $F$ is vertical, so no components are needed.
Step 6Sum forces in x: $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x = 0$$ since no horizontal load is applied.
Step 7Sum forces in y: $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y - F = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 600\ \mathrm{N}$$
Step 8Sum moments about A: point $A$ is chosen because $A_x$ and $A_y$ pass through it; the arm of $F$ is $2.5\ \mathrm{m}$ and $F$ turns the beam clockwise. $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad M_A - F(L) = 0$$
Step 9Substitute numbers with units: $$M_A = (600\ \mathrm{N})(2.5\ \mathrm{m}) = 1500\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}$$
Step 10Convert units: $1500\ \mathrm{N\cdot m} = 1.50\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$.
Step 11State the result: $M_A = 1.50\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$, counterclockwise as assumed, opposing the clockwise tendency of the load.
Step 12Check reasonableness: the wall must supply a moment equal and opposite to force times distance; doubling the length would double $M_A$. Units are force times length.
Worked Solution
Free-body diagram of the cantilever beam
Given: $L = 2.5\ \mathrm{m}$, $F = 600\ \mathrm{N}$ downward at $B$. Find: $M_A$. A fixed support supplies three reactions: $A_x$, $A_y$ and a moment $M_A$. $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x = 0$$ $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y - 600 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 600\ \mathrm{N}$$ $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad M_A - (600\ \mathrm{N})(2.5\ \mathrm{m}) = 0$$ $$M_A = 1500\ \mathrm{N\cdot m} = 1.50\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}\ \text{(counterclockwise)}$$
Problem 5
Ch. 5 — Cantilever Beam Including Its Own Weight
A uniform cantilevered beam $AB$ of length $4\ \mathrm{m}$ and mass $100\ \mathrm{kg}$ is fixed to a wall at its left end $A$. A vertical downward force $F = 2\ \mathrm{kN}$ acts at the free end $B$. Use $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.
Uniform cantilever beam with an end load
Determine the reaction moment $M_A$ at the fixed support.
Solution
9.96 kN·m
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagram of the uniform cantilever
Step 1Draw the free-body diagram: isolate beam $AB$. At the fixed end $A$ show $A_x$, $A_y$ and the moment $M_A$ (assumed counterclockwise). Show the weight $W$ acting down at the midpoint $G$ and the force $F = 2\ \mathrm{kN}$ acting down at $B$.
Step 2List knowns and unknowns: $L = 4\ \mathrm{m}$, $m = 100\ \mathrm{kg}$, $F = 2\ \mathrm{kN}$, $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Unknowns: $A_x$, $A_y$, $M_A$; wanted $M_A$.
Step 3State assumptions: the beam is rigid and uniform, so its whole weight can be treated as one force at the midpoint, $2\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$.
Step 4Set axes and sign convention: take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive.
Step 5Convert units: $F = 2\ \mathrm{kN} = 2000\ \mathrm{N}$ so that every force is in newtons.
Step 6Compute the weight: $$W = mg = (100\ \mathrm{kg})(9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}) = 981\ \mathrm{N}$$
Step 7Sum forces in x: $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x = 0$$ because no horizontal load acts (all loads are vertical, so no components are needed).
Step 8Sum forces in y: $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y - W - F = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 981 + 2000 = 2981\ \mathrm{N}$$
Step 9Sum moments about A: point $A$ removes $A_x$ and $A_y$. Arms: $W$ at $2\ \mathrm{m}$, $F$ at $4\ \mathrm{m}$; both turn the beam clockwise. $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad M_A - W(2) - F(4) = 0$$
Step 10Substitute numbers with units: $$M_A = (981\ \mathrm{N})(2\ \mathrm{m}) + (2000\ \mathrm{N})(4\ \mathrm{m}) = 1962 + 8000$$
Step 11Solve for M_A: $M_A = 9962\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}$.
Step 12State the result: $M_A = 9.96\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$, counterclockwise.
Step 13Check reasonableness: the beam weight adds $1962\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}$, about $20\%$ of the total, so neglecting it would be a noticeable error here. Units are $\mathrm{N\cdot m}$.
Worked Solution
Free-body diagram of the uniform cantilever
Given: $L = 4\ \mathrm{m}$, $m = 100\ \mathrm{kg}$, $F = 2\ \mathrm{kN} = 2000\ \mathrm{N}$ at $B$, $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find: $M_A$. Weight of the beam, acting at its midpoint $G$ ($2\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$): $$W = mg = (100\ \mathrm{kg})(9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}) = 981\ \mathrm{N}$$ $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x = 0$$ $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y - 981 - 2000 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 2981\ \mathrm{N}$$ $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad M_A - (981\ \mathrm{N})(2\ \mathrm{m}) - (2000\ \mathrm{N})(4\ \mathrm{m}) = 0$$ $$M_A = 1962 + 8000 = 9962\ \mathrm{N\cdot m} = 9.96\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}\ \text{(counterclockwise)}$$
Problem 6
Ch. 5 — Cantilever with Inclined Force, Couple and Weight
A uniform cantilevered beam $AB$ of length $5\ \mathrm{m}$ and mass $80\ \mathrm{kg}$ is fixed at its left end $A$. It carries: a clockwise couple moment $M_D = 2\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$ applied at $D$, $2\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$; a vertical downward force $F_C = 1.5\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $C$, $3\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$; and a force $F_B = 3\ \mathrm{kN}$ at the free end $B$, directed downward and to the left at $40^{\circ}$ below the horizontal. Neglect the depth of the beam. Use $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.
Cantilever with a couple, a vertical load and an inclined end load
Determine the reaction moment $M_A$ at the fixed support.
Solution
18.1 kN·m
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagram of the loaded cantilever
Step 1Draw the free-body diagram: isolate beam $AB$. At the fixed end show $A_x$ (right), $A_y$ (up) and $M_A$ (counterclockwise). Show the weight $W$ down at the midpoint $G$, the clockwise couple $M_D$ at $D$, $F_C = 1.5\ \mathrm{kN}$ down at $C$, and $F_B = 3\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $B$ pointing down-left at $40^{\circ}$.
Step 2List knowns and unknowns: $L = 5\ \mathrm{m}$, $m = 80\ \mathrm{kg}$, $M_D = 2\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$, $F_C = 1.5\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $3\ \mathrm{m}$, $F_B = 3\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $40^{\circ}$. Unknowns: $A_x$, $A_y$, $M_A$; wanted $M_A$.
Step 3State assumptions: rigid uniform beam (weight at midpoint, $2.5\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$); depth neglected, so all forces act on the beam axis.
Step 4Set axes and sign convention: take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive.
Step 5Compute the weight in kN: $$W = mg = (80\ \mathrm{kg})(9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}) = 784.8\ \mathrm{N} = 0.7848\ \mathrm{kN}$$
Step 6Resolve F_B in x: $$F_{Bx} = 3\cos 40^{\circ} = 3(0.7660) = 2.298\ \mathrm{kN}\ \text{(to the left)}$$
Step 7Resolve F_B in y: $$F_{By} = 3\sin 40^{\circ} = 3(0.6428) = 1.928\ \mathrm{kN}\ \text{(downward)}$$
Step 8Sum forces in x: $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x - F_{Bx} = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_x = 2.30\ \mathrm{kN}\ \text{(to the right)}$$
Step 9Sum forces in y: $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y - W - F_C - F_{By} = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 0.7848 + 1.5 + 1.928 = 4.21\ \mathrm{kN}$$
Step 10Identify moment arms about A: point $A$ removes $A_x$ and $A_y$. $W$ acts at $2.5\ \mathrm{m}$, $F_C$ at $3\ \mathrm{m}$, $F_{By}$ at $5\ \mathrm{m}$; $F_{Bx}$ acts along the beam axis through $A$, so its arm is zero. The couple $M_D$ enters with its full value no matter where it is applied.
Step 11Sum moments about A: every load turns the beam clockwise, so $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad M_A - W(2.5) - F_C(3) - M_D - F_{By}(5) = 0$$
Step 12Substitute numbers with units: $$M_A = (0.7848)(2.5) + (1.5)(3) + 2 + (1.928)(5)\ \ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$$
Step 13Evaluate each term: $$M_A = 1.962 + 4.500 + 2.000 + 9.642 = 18.10\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$$
Step 14State the result: $M_A = 18.1\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$, counterclockwise as assumed.
Step 15Check reasonableness: the inclined end load supplies more than half of the moment because it has the longest arm; the couple's $2\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$ did not depend on its $2\ \mathrm{m}$ location. All terms have units of $\mathrm{kN\cdot m}$.
Worked Solution
Free-body diagram of the loaded cantilever
Given: $L = 5\ \mathrm{m}$, $m = 80\ \mathrm{kg}$; $M_D = 2\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$ clockwise; $F_C = 1.5\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $3\ \mathrm{m}$; $F_B = 3\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $5\ \mathrm{m}$, $40^{\circ}$ below horizontal, down-left. Find: $M_A$. $$W = (80)(9.81) = 784.8\ \mathrm{N} = 0.7848\ \mathrm{kN}\ \text{at } 2.5\ \mathrm{m}$$ $$F_{Bx} = 3\cos 40^{\circ} = 2.298\ \mathrm{kN}\ (\leftarrow),\qquad F_{By} = 3\sin 40^{\circ} = 1.928\ \mathrm{kN}\ (\downarrow)$$ $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x - 2.298 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_x = 2.30\ \mathrm{kN}$$ $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y - 0.7848 - 1.5 - 1.928 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 4.21\ \mathrm{kN}$$ The line of action of $F_{Bx}$ passes through $A$, so it has no moment about $A$. A couple has the same moment about every point. $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad M_A - 0.7848(2.5) - 1.5(3) - 2 - 1.928(5) = 0$$ $$M_A = 1.962 + 4.500 + 2.000 + 9.642 = 18.1\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}\ \text{(counterclockwise)}$$

Ch. 5 — Plank and Person: Masses to Weights

Person standing on a simply supported plank
Determine the vertical reaction $A_y$ at the pin.
Solution
638 N
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagram of the plank
Step 1Draw the free-body diagram: isolate the plank. Show $A_x$ and $A_y$ at the pin $A$, $B_y$ (up) at the roller $B$, the person's weight $W_p$ down at $C$, and the plank's weight $W_b$ down at its midpoint $G$.
Step 2List knowns and unknowns: $L = 4\ \mathrm{m}$, $m_b = 40\ \mathrm{kg}$, $m_p = 60\ \mathrm{kg}$, $AC = 1\ \mathrm{m}$, $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Unknowns: $A_x$, $A_y$, $B_y$; wanted $A_y$.
Step 3State assumptions: the plank is rigid and uniform (weight at midpoint, $2\ \mathrm{m}$ from each end); the person is treated as a point load at $C$.
Step 4Set axes and sign convention: take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive.
Step 5Convert the person's mass to weight: $$W_p = m_p g = (60\ \mathrm{kg})(9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}) = 588.6\ \mathrm{N}$$
Step 6Convert the plank's mass to weight: $$W_b = m_b g = (40\ \mathrm{kg})(9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}) = 392.4\ \mathrm{N}$$
Step 7Sum forces in x: $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x = 0$$ since all loads are vertical and no components are needed.
Step 8Sum moments about B: point $B$ is chosen because it eliminates $B_y$ and leaves $A_y$ as the only unknown. Arms from $B$: $A_y$ at $4\ \mathrm{m}$, $W_p$ at $3\ \mathrm{m}$, $W_b$ at $2\ \mathrm{m}$. $$\sum M_B = 0:\quad -A_y(4) + W_p(3) + W_b(2) = 0$$
Step 9Rearrange for the unknown: $$A_y = \frac{W_p(3) + W_b(2)}{4}$$
Step 10Substitute numbers with units: $$A_y = \frac{(588.6\ \mathrm{N})(3\ \mathrm{m}) + (392.4\ \mathrm{N})(2\ \mathrm{m})}{4\ \mathrm{m}} = \frac{2550.6\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}}{4\ \mathrm{m}}$$

Step 12Sum forces in y as a check: $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y + B_y - W_p - W_b = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; B_y = 981.0 - 637.7 = 343\ \mathrm{N}$$
Step 13Check reasonableness: the person stands nearer $A$, so $A$ carries more than half of the $981\ \mathrm{N}$ total, which it does ($638\ \mathrm{N}$ versus $343\ \mathrm{N}$).

Problem 9
Ch. 5 — L-Shaped Member with a Roller on an Incline
The rigid L-shaped member $ACB$ has a horizontal arm $AC = 4\ \mathrm{m}$ and a vertical leg $CB = 1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ that extends downward from $C$. It is supported by a pin at $A$ (left end of the arm) and by a roller at $B$ (bottom of the leg) that rests on a smooth surface inclined at $30^{\circ}$ to the horizontal. The normal reaction $N_B$ at the roller therefore acts at $30^{\circ}$ from the vertical, pointing upward and to the left. A vertical downward force $F = 5\ \mathrm{kN}$ acts on the arm at $D$, $2\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Neglect the weight of the member.
L-shaped member with a pin at A and a roller on a 30 degree incline at B
Determine the normal reaction $N_B$ at the roller.
Solution
3.68 kN
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagram of the L-shaped member
Step 1Draw the free-body diagram: isolate the L-shaped member. Show $A_x$ (assumed right) and $A_y$ (assumed up) at the pin $A$, $F = 5\ \mathrm{kN}$ down at $D$, and the roller force $N_B$ at $B$ drawn perpendicular to the incline, pointing up and to the left at $30^{\circ}$ from the vertical.
Step 2List knowns and unknowns: $AC = 4\ \mathrm{m}$, $CB = 1.5\ \mathrm{m}$, $AD = 2\ \mathrm{m}$, $F = 5\ \mathrm{kN}$, incline angle $30^{\circ}$. Unknowns: $A_x$, $A_y$, $N_B$; wanted $N_B$.
Step 3State assumptions: rigid, weightless member; smooth incline, so the roller reaction is purely normal to the surface. A surface tilted $30^{\circ}$ from horizontal has a normal tilted $30^{\circ}$ from vertical.
Step 4Set axes and sign convention: take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive.
Step 5Resolve N_B in x: $$N_{Bx} = N_B\sin 30^{\circ} = 0.500\,N_B\ \text{(to the left)}$$
Step 6Resolve N_B in y: $$N_{By} = N_B\cos 30^{\circ} = 0.8660\,N_B\ \text{(upward)}$$
Step 7Locate B relative to A: $B$ is $4\ \mathrm{m}$ to the right of $A$ and $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ below it. So $N_{By}$ has a horizontal arm of $4\ \mathrm{m}$ (counterclockwise about $A$) and $N_{Bx}$ has a vertical arm of $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ (a leftward force below $A$ turns clockwise).
Step 8Sum moments about A: point $A$ is chosen because it eliminates $A_x$ and $A_y$, leaving $N_B$ alone; $F$ has an arm of $2\ \mathrm{m}$. $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad N_B\cos 30^{\circ}(4) - N_B\sin 30^{\circ}(1.5) - F(2) = 0$$
Step 9Factor out the unknown: $$N_B\left[(0.8660)(4) - (0.500)(1.5)\right] = N_B(3.464 - 0.750) = N_B(2.714\ \mathrm{m})$$
Step 10Substitute numbers with units: $$N_B = \frac{(5\ \mathrm{kN})(2\ \mathrm{m})}{2.714\ \mathrm{m}}$$
Step 11State the result: $N_B = 3.68\ \mathrm{kN}$, acting up and to the left at $30^{\circ}$ from the vertical.
Step 12Sum forces in x: $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x - N_B\sin 30^{\circ} = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_x = 3.684(0.500) = 1.84\ \mathrm{kN}\ \text{(to the right)}$$
Step 13Sum forces in y: $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y + N_B\cos 30^{\circ} - F = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 5 - 3.19 = 1.81\ \mathrm{kN}\ \text{(upward)}$$
Step 14Check reasonableness: on a level surface the roller would carry $5(2)/4 = 2.50\ \mathrm{kN}$; the tilted reaction must be larger ($3.68\ \mathrm{kN}$) because part of it acts horizontally and works against itself in the moment balance. Units: $\mathrm{kN\cdot m/m = kN}$.
Worked Solution
Free-body diagram of the L-shaped member
Given: $AC = 4\ \mathrm{m}$, $CB = 1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ (down), $F = 5\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $2\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$, incline $30^{\circ}$. Find: $N_B$. The roller reaction is perpendicular to the incline, i.e. $30^{\circ}$ from the vertical: $$N_{Bx} = N_B\sin 30^{\circ}\ (\leftarrow),\qquad N_{By} = N_B\cos 30^{\circ}\ (\uparrow)$$ Moments about $A$. $B$ is $4\ \mathrm{m}$ to the right of and $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ below $A$, so $N_{By}$ (arm $4\ \mathrm{m}$) is counterclockwise and $N_{Bx}$ (arm $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$) is clockwise: $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad N_B\cos 30^{\circ}(4) - N_B\sin 30^{\circ}(1.5) - 5(2) = 0$$ $$N_B(3.464 - 0.750) = 10 \;\Rightarrow\; N_B = \frac{10\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}}{2.714\ \mathrm{m}} = 3.68\ \mathrm{kN}$$ $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x - N_B\sin 30^{\circ} = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_x = 1.84\ \mathrm{kN}$$ $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y + N_B\cos 30^{\circ} - 5 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 5 - 3.19 = 1.81\ \mathrm{kN}$$

Problem 12
Ch. 5 — Inclined Ladder: Pin at the Floor, Smooth Wall
A uniform ladder $AB$ of length $5\ \mathrm{m}$ and mass $20\ \mathrm{kg}$ leans against a smooth vertical wall, making an angle of $60^{\circ}$ with the horizontal floor. Its lower end $A$ is held by a pin (a hinged foot bolted to the floor), and its upper end $B$ rests against the wall. A person of mass $75\ \mathrm{kg}$ stands on the ladder at $D$, $3.5\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$ measured along the ladder. Use $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.
Ladder pinned at the floor and resting on a smooth wall
Determine the magnitude of the pin reaction at $A$.
Solution
997 N
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagram of the ladder
Step 1Draw the free-body diagram: isolate the ladder. Show $A_x$ (assumed right, toward the wall) and $A_y$ (assumed up) at the pin $A$, the wall force $N_B$ at $B$ pointing horizontally to the left (away from the wall), the ladder weight $W_l$ down at the midpoint $G$, and the person's weight $W_p$ down at $D$.
Step 2List knowns and unknowns: $L = 5\ \mathrm{m}$, angle $60^{\circ}$, $m_l = 20\ \mathrm{kg}$, $m_p = 75\ \mathrm{kg}$, $AD = 3.5\ \mathrm{m}$, $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Unknowns: $A_x$, $A_y$, $N_B$; wanted $|A|$.
Step 3State assumptions: rigid uniform ladder (weight at $2.5\ \mathrm{m}$ along it); smooth wall, so its reaction is perpendicular to the wall (horizontal) with no vertical component.
Step 4Set axes and sign convention: take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive.
Step 5Compute the ladder weight: $$W_l = (20\ \mathrm{kg})(9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}) = 196.2\ \mathrm{N}$$
Step 6Compute the person's weight: $$W_p = (75\ \mathrm{kg})(9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}) = 735.75\ \mathrm{N}$$
Step 7Find the horizontal arms of the weights about A: the forces themselves need no resolving (each is vertical or horizontal), but distances along the ladder must be projected. $$d_l = 2.5\cos 60^{\circ} = 1.25\ \mathrm{m},\qquad d_p = 3.5\cos 60^{\circ} = 1.75\ \mathrm{m}$$
Step 8Find the vertical arm of N_B about A: $$h = 5\sin 60^{\circ} = 4.330\ \mathrm{m}$$
Step 9Sum moments about A: point $A$ removes both pin components. $N_B$ (leftward, above $A$) turns counterclockwise; the weights turn clockwise. $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad N_B(h) - W_l(d_l) - W_p(d_p) = 0$$
Step 10Substitute numbers with units: $$N_B = \frac{(196.2\ \mathrm{N})(1.25\ \mathrm{m}) + (735.75\ \mathrm{N})(1.75\ \mathrm{m})}{4.330\ \mathrm{m}} = \frac{1532.8\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}}{4.330\ \mathrm{m}}$$
Step 11Solve for N_B: $N_B = 354.0\ \mathrm{N}$, to the left.
Step 12Sum forces in x: $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x - N_B = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_x = 354.0\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(to the right)}$$
Step 13Sum forces in y: $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y - W_l - W_p = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 196.2 + 735.75 = 931.95\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(upward)}$$
Step 14Combine the pin components: $$|A| = \sqrt{A_x^2 + A_y^2} = \sqrt{(354.0)^2 + (931.95)^2} = 997\ \mathrm{N}$$
Step 15State the result: the pin reaction at $A$ is $997\ \mathrm{N}$, directed up and to the right at $\tan^{-1}(931.95/354.0) = 69.2^{\circ}$ above the horizontal.
Step 16Check reasonableness: the floor carries all of the weight ($932\ \mathrm{N}$) because the smooth wall cannot push vertically, and the horizontal pin force equals the wall force. The reaction is steeper than the ladder ($69.2^{\circ}$ versus $60^{\circ}$), as it must be for the three force lines to balance.

Free-body diagram of the ladder

Free-body diagram of the beam with the roller at its limit

A rigid right-angle bell crank is pinned at its corner $O$. Right-angle bell crank pinned at O
Determine the magnitude of the pin reaction at $O$.
Solution
961 N
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagram of the bell crank
Step 1Draw the free-body diagram: isolate the bell crank from its pin. Show $O_x$ (assumed right) and $O_y$ (assumed up) at $O$, $F = 800\ \mathrm{N}$ down at $A$, and the horizontal force $P$ at $B$, drawn to the left.
Step 2List knowns and unknowns: $OA = 0.2\ \mathrm{m}$, $OB = 0.3\ \mathrm{m}$, $F = 800\ \mathrm{N}$. Unknowns: $P$, $O_x$, $O_y$; wanted $|O| = \sqrt{O_x^2 + O_y^2}$.
Step 3State assumptions: rigid, weightless crank; frictionless pin, so the pin supplies force components but no moment.
Step 4Set axes and sign convention: take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive.
Step 5Check for angled forces: $F$ is vertical and $P$ is horizontal, so no force needs to be resolved.
Step 6Decide the direction of P: $F$ acts $0.2\ \mathrm{m}$ to the right of $O$ and turns the crank clockwise, so $P$ must turn it counterclockwise; a force above $O$ does that when it points to the left.
Step 7Sum moments about O: point $O$ removes $O_x$ and $O_y$. The arm of $F$ is $0.2\ \mathrm{m}$ and the arm of $P$ is $0.3\ \mathrm{m}$. $$\sum M_O = 0:\quad P(0.3) - F(0.2) = 0$$

Step 9Solve for P: $P = 533.3\ \mathrm{N}$, to the left.
Step 10Sum forces in x: $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad O_x - P = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; O_x = 533.3\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(to the right)}$$
Step 11Sum forces in y: $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad O_y - F = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; O_y = 800\ \mathrm{N}\ \text{(upward)}$$
Step 12Combine the pin components: $$|O| = \sqrt{O_x^2 + O_y^2} = \sqrt{(533.3)^2 + (800)^2} = 961\ \mathrm{N}$$

Problem 20
Ch. 5 — Finding a Beam's Mass from a Measured Reaction
A uniform beam $AB$ of length $4\ \mathrm{m}$ and unknown mass $m$ is supported by a pin at $A$ (left end) and by a roller at $B$ (right end) that sits on a load cell. A crate of mass $50\ \mathrm{kg}$ rests on the beam at $C$, $1\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$. The load cell shows that the vertical reaction at $B$ is $520\ \mathrm{N}$. Use $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.
Beam with a crate, with the roller reaction measured by a load cell
Determine the mass $m$ of the beam.
Solution
81.0 kg
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagram of the beam with the measured reaction
Step 1Draw the free-body diagram: isolate the beam. Show $A_x$ and $A_y$ at the pin $A$, the measured roller force $B_y = 520\ \mathrm{N}$ up at $B$, the crate's weight $W_c$ down at $C$, and the beam's unknown weight $W = mg$ down at the midpoint $G$.
Step 2List knowns and unknowns: $L = 4\ \mathrm{m}$, crate $50\ \mathrm{kg}$ at $AC = 1\ \mathrm{m}$, $B_y = 520\ \mathrm{N}$, $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Unknowns: $W$ (hence $m$), $A_x$, $A_y$; wanted $m$.
Step 3State assumptions: rigid uniform beam, so its weight acts $2\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$; the crate is a point load; the load cell reads the full vertical roller reaction.
Step 4Set axes and sign convention: take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive.
Step 5Compute the crate's weight: $$W_c = (50\ \mathrm{kg})(9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}) = 490.5\ \mathrm{N}$$
Step 6Sum forces in x: $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x = 0$$ because all loads are vertical (nothing to resolve).
Step 7Sum moments about A: point $A$ removes the two unknown pin components, leaving $W$ as the only unknown. Arms: $W_c$ at $1\ \mathrm{m}$, $W$ at $2\ \mathrm{m}$, $B_y$ at $4\ \mathrm{m}$. $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad B_y(4) - W_c(1) - W(2) = 0$$
Step 8Rearrange for the unknown weight: $$W = \frac{B_y(4) - W_c(1)}{2}$$
Step 9Substitute numbers with units: $$W = \frac{(520\ \mathrm{N})(4\ \mathrm{m}) - (490.5\ \mathrm{N})(1\ \mathrm{m})}{2\ \mathrm{m}} = \frac{1589.5\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}}{2\ \mathrm{m}}$$
Step 10Solve for the weight: $W = 794.75\ \mathrm{N}$.
Step 11Convert weight to mass: $$m = \frac{W}{g} = \frac{794.75\ \mathrm{N}}{9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}}$$
Step 12State the result: $m = 81.0\ \mathrm{kg}$.
Step 13Sum forces in y: $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y + B_y - W_c - W = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; A_y = 490.5 + 794.75 - 520 = 765\ \mathrm{N}$$
Step 14Check reasonableness: the beam alone would put $794.75/2 = 397\ \mathrm{N}$ on $B$, and the crate (one quarter of the way along) adds $490.5/4 = 123\ \mathrm{N}$, giving $520\ \mathrm{N}$ as measured. Units: $\mathrm{N/(m/s^2) = kg}$.
Worked Solution
Free-body diagram of the beam with the measured reaction
Given: $L = 4\ \mathrm{m}$, crate $50\ \mathrm{kg}$ at $1\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$, measured $B_y = 520\ \mathrm{N}$, $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find: beam mass $m$. $$W_c = 50(9.81) = 490.5\ \mathrm{N}$$ The beam weight $W = mg$ acts at midspan, $2\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$. $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad 520(4) - 490.5(1) - W(2) = 0$$ $$W = \frac{2080 - 490.5}{2} = \frac{1589.5\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}}{2\ \mathrm{m}} = 794.75\ \mathrm{N}$$ $$m = \frac{W}{g} = \frac{794.75\ \mathrm{N}}{9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}} = 81.0\ \mathrm{kg}$$ $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad A_x = 0,\qquad \sum F_y = 0:\quad A_y = 490.5 + 794.75 - 520 = 765\ \mathrm{N}$$
