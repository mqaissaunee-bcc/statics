Problem 1
Ch. 7 — Internal Shear at a Point
A simply supported beam $AB$ of length $6\ \mathrm{m}$ has a pin at $A$ and a roller at $B$. A single downward point load $P = 12\ \mathrm{kN}$ acts $2\ \mathrm{m}$ to the right of $A$. Point $C$ lies on the beam $4\ \mathrm{m}$ to the right of $A$. Neglect the weight of the beam. Use the sign convention from class: normal force $N$ is positive in tension, shear force $V$ is positive when it tends to rotate the segment clockwise, and bending moment $M$ is positive when it bends the segment concave up.
Simply supported beam with one point load and section point C
Determine the internal shear force $V_C$ at point $C$.
Solution
-4.00 kN
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagrams of the whole beam and of segment CB
Step 1Draw the free-body diagrams: Isolate the whole beam from its supports. The forces on it are the applied load $P = 12\ \mathrm{kN}$ downward at $2\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$, the pin reactions $A_x$ and $A_y$ at $A$, and the roller reaction $B_y$ (vertical) at $B$.
Step 2List knowns and unknown: Span $= 6\ \mathrm{m}$; $P = 12\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $2\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$; point $C$ is $4\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$, so it is $2\ \mathrm{m}$ from $B$. Unknown: internal shear $V_C$.
Step 3Set axes and sign convention: Take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive; internal loads follow the class convention ($N$ positive in tension, $V$ positive clockwise, $M$ positive concave up).
Step 4Sum forces in x on the whole beam: No applied load has a horizontal component, so $\sum F_x = 0$ gives $A_x = 0$.
Step 5Sum moments about A: Point $A$ is chosen because $A_x$ and $A_y$ pass through it and drop out. The moment arms are $2\ \mathrm{m}$ for $P$ and $6\ \mathrm{m}$ for $B_y$: $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad B_y(6) - P(2) = 0$$
Step 6Substitute numbers: $$B_y(6\ \mathrm{m}) - (12\ \mathrm{kN})(2\ \mathrm{m}) = 0$$
Step 7Solve for the roller reaction: $$B_y = \frac{24\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}}{6\ \mathrm{m}} = 4.00\ \mathrm{kN}\ \text{(upward)}$$
Step 8Cut the beam at C: Keep the right segment $CB$ because it carries the fewest loads (only $B_y$). The cut exposes $N_C$, $V_C$ and $M_C$; on the left face of a segment, positive shear $V_C$ is drawn upward (clockwise tendency).
Step 9Sum forces in y on segment CB: The segment is in equilibrium, so $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad V_C + B_y = 0$$
Step 10Substitute numbers: $$V_C + 4.00\ \mathrm{kN} = 0$$
Step 11Solve for the shear: $$V_C = -4.00\ \mathrm{kN}$$
Step 12State the result: $V_C = -4.00\ \mathrm{kN}$. The negative sign means the shear acts opposite to the assumed positive direction, with magnitude $4.00\ \mathrm{kN}$.
Step 13Check with the left segment: From $\sum F_y = 0$ on the whole beam, $A_y = 12 - 4.00 = 8.00\ \mathrm{kN}$. On segment $AC$, $V_C = A_y - P = 8.00 - 12 = -4.00\ \mathrm{kN}$, which agrees; the units are force (kN).
Worked Solution
Free-body diagrams of the whole beam and of segment CB
**Given:** span $6\ \mathrm{m}$, $P = 12\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $2\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$, point $C$ at $4\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$. **Find:** $V_C$. **Reactions (whole beam):** $\sum M_A = 0:\ B_y(6\ \mathrm{m}) - (12\ \mathrm{kN})(2\ \mathrm{m}) = 0 \Rightarrow B_y = 4.00\ \mathrm{kN}$. $\sum F_x = 0 \Rightarrow A_x = 0$. **Section at $C$, right segment $CB$:** only $B_y$ acts on it. With $V_C$ drawn upward on the left (cut) face (positive shear), $\sum F_y = 0:\ V_C + B_y = 0 \Rightarrow V_C = -4.00\ \mathrm{kN}$. **Check with the left segment:** $A_y = 12 - 4 = 8\ \mathrm{kN}$ and $V_C = 8 - 12 = -4.00\ \mathrm{kN}$. The negative sign means the shear acts opposite to the positive direction assumed.
Problem 2
Ch. 7 — Internal Bending Moment Under a Uniform Load
A simply supported floor beam $AB$ spans $8\ \mathrm{m}$ between a pin at $A$ and a roller at $B$. It carries a uniform distributed load $w = 3\ \mathrm{kN/m}$ over its entire length. Point $C$ is $3\ \mathrm{m}$ to the right of $A$. Neglect the weight of the beam. Use the sign convention from class: normal force $N$ is positive in tension, shear force $V$ is positive when it tends to rotate the segment clockwise, and bending moment $M$ is positive when it bends the segment concave up.
Simply supported beam under a uniform distributed load
Determine the internal bending moment $M_C$ at point $C$.
Solution
22.5 kN*m
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagrams of the whole beam and of segment AC
Step 1Draw the free-body diagrams: Isolate the whole beam. It carries the distributed load $w = 3\ \mathrm{kN/m}$ over $8\ \mathrm{m}$, the pin reactions $A_x$, $A_y$ at $A$, and the roller reaction $B_y$ at $B$.
Step 2List knowns and unknown: $L = 8\ \mathrm{m}$, $w = 3\ \mathrm{kN/m}$, $C$ is $3\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Unknown: bending moment $M_C$.
Step 3Set axes and sign convention: Take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive; internal loads follow the class convention ($N$ positive in tension, $V$ positive clockwise, $M$ positive concave up).
Step 4Replace the distributed load for the reaction calculation only: $$F_R = wL = (3\ \mathrm{kN/m})(8\ \mathrm{m}) = 24\ \mathrm{kN}$$ acting at midspan, $4\ \mathrm{m}$ from each support.
Step 5Sum forces in x on the whole beam: There are no horizontal loads, so $\sum F_x = 0$ gives $A_x = 0$.
Step 6Sum moments about B: Point $B$ is chosen so that $B_y$ drops out and $A_y$ is found directly. Arms: $8\ \mathrm{m}$ for $A_y$, $4\ \mathrm{m}$ for $F_R$: $$\sum M_B = 0:\quad -A_y(8) + F_R(4) = 0$$
Step 7Substitute numbers: $$-A_y(8\ \mathrm{m}) + (24\ \mathrm{kN})(4\ \mathrm{m}) = 0$$
Step 8Solve for the pin reaction: $$A_y = \frac{96\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}}{8\ \mathrm{m}} = 12.0\ \mathrm{kN}$$
Step 9Cut the beam at C: Keep the left segment $AC$ ($3\ \mathrm{m}$ long). It carries $A_y$, the part of the distributed load on those $3\ \mathrm{m}$, and the exposed $N_C$, $V_C$ (downward on the right face) and $M_C$ (counterclockwise on the right face).
Step 10Find the load on the segment: The distributed load must be re-evaluated for the cut piece: $$F_{AC} = (3\ \mathrm{kN/m})(3\ \mathrm{m}) = 9\ \mathrm{kN}$$ acting at the middle of the segment, $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ from $C$.
Step 11Sum moments about the cut C: Taking moments at the cut eliminates $N_C$ and $V_C$. Arms: $3\ \mathrm{m}$ for $A_y$ (clockwise), $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ for $F_{AC}$ (counterclockwise): $$\sum M_C = 0:\quad M_C - A_y(3) + F_{AC}(1.5) = 0$$
Step 12Substitute numbers: $$M_C - (12.0\ \mathrm{kN})(3\ \mathrm{m}) + (9\ \mathrm{kN})(1.5\ \mathrm{m}) = 0$$
Step 13Solve for the moment: $$M_C = 36.0 - 13.5 = 22.5\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$$
Step 14State the result: $M_C = 22.5\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$, positive, so the beam bends concave up at $C$.
Step 15Check for reasonableness: The midspan moment is $wL^2/8 = (3)(8)^2/8 = 24\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$; $C$ is near midspan and $22.5 < 24$, as expected. Units are force times length.
Worked Solution
Free-body diagrams of the whole beam and of segment AC
**Given:** $L = 8\ \mathrm{m}$, $w = 3\ \mathrm{kN/m}$, $C$ at $3\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$. **Find:** $M_C$. **Reactions:** the load resultant is $F_R = wL = (3)(8) = 24\ \mathrm{kN}$ at midspan. $\sum M_B = 0:\ -A_y(8) + 24(4) = 0 \Rightarrow A_y = 12.0\ \mathrm{kN}$; $A_x = 0$. **Section at $C$, left segment $AC$:** the load on the segment is $(3\ \mathrm{kN/m})(3\ \mathrm{m}) = 9\ \mathrm{kN}$ acting $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ from $C$. $\sum M_C = 0:\ M_C - A_y(3) + 9(1.5) = 0$. $M_C = (12.0)(3) - (9)(1.5) = 36.0 - 13.5 = 22.5\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$ (positive: concave up).
Problem 3
Ch. 7 — Section Moment with an Inclined Load and a Partial Distributed Load
A simply supported beam $AB$ is $10\ \mathrm{m}$ long with a pin at $A$ and a roller at $B$. A force $F = 20\ \mathrm{kN}$ is applied $4\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$; it acts downward and to the left, making an angle of $60^{\circ}$ with the beam axis. A uniform distributed load $w = 2\ \mathrm{kN/m}$ acts over the last $4\ \mathrm{m}$ of the beam (from $6\ \mathrm{m}$ to $10\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$). Point $C$ is $7\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Neglect the weight of the beam. Use the sign convention from class: normal force $N$ is positive in tension, shear force $V$ is positive when it tends to rotate the segment clockwise, and bending moment $M$ is positive when it bends the segment concave up.
Beam with an inclined force and a partial uniform load
Determine the internal bending moment $M_C$ at point $C$.
Solution
31.0 kN*m
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagrams of the whole beam and of segment CB
Step 1Draw the free-body diagrams: Isolate the whole beam. Forces: the inclined force $F = 20\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $4\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$, the distributed load $w = 2\ \mathrm{kN/m}$ on the last $4\ \mathrm{m}$, pin reactions $A_x$, $A_y$ at $A$, and roller reaction $B_y$ at $B$.
Step 2List knowns and unknown: $L = 10\ \mathrm{m}$; $F = 20\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $60^{\circ}$ to the beam axis; $w = 2\ \mathrm{kN/m}$ from $x = 6\ \mathrm{m}$ to $x = 10\ \mathrm{m}$; $C$ at $x = 7\ \mathrm{m}$. Unknown: $M_C$.
Step 3Set axes and sign convention: Take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive; internal loads follow the class convention ($N$ positive in tension, $V$ positive clockwise, $M$ positive concave up).
Step 4Resolve F into its x component: $$F_x = F\cos 60^{\circ} = (20)(0.500) = 10.0\ \mathrm{kN}\ \text{(to the left)}$$
Step 5Resolve F into its y component: $$F_y = F\sin 60^{\circ} = (20)(0.8660) = 17.32\ \mathrm{kN}\ \text{(downward)}$$
Step 6Replace the distributed load for the reaction calculation: $$F_R = (2\ \mathrm{kN/m})(4\ \mathrm{m}) = 8\ \mathrm{kN}$$ at the middle of the loaded length, $8\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$.
Step 7Sum moments about A: Point $A$ removes $A_x$ and $A_y$; $F_x$ acts along the beam axis through $A$ and has no moment arm. Arms: $4\ \mathrm{m}$ for $F_y$, $8\ \mathrm{m}$ for $F_R$, $10\ \mathrm{m}$ for $B_y$: $$\sum M_A = 0:\quad B_y(10) - F_y(4) - F_R(8) = 0$$
Step 8Substitute numbers: $$B_y(10\ \mathrm{m}) - (17.32\ \mathrm{kN})(4\ \mathrm{m}) - (8\ \mathrm{kN})(8\ \mathrm{m}) = 0$$
Step 9Solve for the roller reaction: $$B_y = \frac{69.28 + 64.0}{10} = 13.33\ \mathrm{kN}$$
Step 10Cut the beam at C: Keep the right segment $CB$, which is $10 - 7 = 3\ \mathrm{m}$ long and carries only $B_y$ and part of the distributed load. On its left (cut) face draw $V_C$ upward, $N_C$ to the left, and $M_C$ clockwise (all positive).
Step 11Find the load on the segment: $$F_{CB} = (2\ \mathrm{kN/m})(3\ \mathrm{m}) = 6\ \mathrm{kN}$$ acting $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ from $C$.
Step 12Sum moments about the cut C: This eliminates $N_C$ and $V_C$. Arms: $3\ \mathrm{m}$ for $B_y$ (counterclockwise), $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ for $F_{CB}$ (clockwise): $$\sum M_C = 0:\quad -M_C + B_y(3) - F_{CB}(1.5) = 0$$
Step 13Substitute numbers: $$-M_C + (13.33\ \mathrm{kN})(3\ \mathrm{m}) - (6\ \mathrm{kN})(1.5\ \mathrm{m}) = 0$$
Step 14Solve for the moment: $$M_C = 39.98 - 9.00 = 31.0\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$$
Step 15State the result: $M_C = 31.0\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$, positive (concave up).
Step 16Check with the left segment: $\sum F_y = 0$ gives $A_y = 17.32 + 8 - 13.33 = 11.99\ \mathrm{kN}$. Then $M_C = A_y(7) - F_y(3) - (2)(1)(0.5) = 83.95 - 51.96 - 1.00 = 31.0\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$, which agrees.
Worked Solution
Free-body diagrams of the whole beam and of segment CB
**Given:** $L = 10\ \mathrm{m}$; $F = 20\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $60^{\circ}$ to the beam, $4\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$; $w = 2\ \mathrm{kN/m}$ from 6 to $10\ \mathrm{m}$; $C$ at $7\ \mathrm{m}$. **Find:** $M_C$. **Components:** $F_x = 20\cos 60^{\circ} = 10.0\ \mathrm{kN}$ (left), $F_y = 20\sin 60^{\circ} = 17.32\ \mathrm{kN}$ (down). **Load resultant:** $(2)(4) = 8\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $8\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$. **Reaction:** $\sum M_A = 0:\ B_y(10) - 17.32(4) - 8(8) = 0 \Rightarrow B_y = 13.33\ \mathrm{kN}$. **Section at $C$, right segment $CB$ ($3\ \mathrm{m}$):** load on segment $= (2)(3) = 6\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ from $C$. $\sum M_C = 0:\ -M_C + B_y(3) - 6(1.5) = 0$. $M_C = (13.33)(3) - 9.00 = 31.0\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$.

Neglect the weight of the beam. Use the sign convention from class: normal force $N$ is positive in tension, shear force $V$ is positive when it tends to rotate the segment clockwise, and bending moment $M$ is positive when it bends the segment concave up.
Cantilever with an inclined force at the free end
Determine the internal normal force $N_C$ at point $C$.

Step 7Resolve F into its y component: $$F_y = F\sin 40^{\circ} = (8)(0.6428) = 5.14\ \mathrm{kN}\ \text{(downward)}$$
Step 8Sum forces in x on segment CB: Only $N_C$ and $F_x$ act along the beam, so $$\sum F_x = 0:\quad -N_C + F_x = 0$$
Step 9Substitute numbers: $$-N_C + 6.13\ \mathrm{kN} = 0$$
Step 10Solve for the normal force: $$N_C = 6.13\ \mathrm{kN}$$
Step 11State the result: $N_C = 6.13\ \mathrm{kN}$, positive, so the beam is in tension at $C$.
Step 12Check for reasonableness: The cable pulls the beam away from the wall, so tension is expected, and $6.13\ \mathrm{kN}$ is less than the full $8\ \mathrm{kN}$ because only the component along the beam stretches it. The same value holds at every section between $A$ and $B$.

Problem 5
Ch. 7 — Section Moment in a Loaded Cantilever
A cantilever balcony beam is fixed to a wall at its left end $A$ and free at its right end $B$; its length is $4\ \mathrm{m}$. It carries a uniform distributed load $w = 5\ \mathrm{kN/m}$ over its whole length and a downward point load $P = 6\ \mathrm{kN}$ at the free end $B$. Point $C$ is $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ from the wall. Neglect the weight of the beam. Use the sign convention from class: normal force $N$ is positive in tension, shear force $V$ is positive when it tends to rotate the segment clockwise, and bending moment $M$ is positive when it bends the segment concave up.
Cantilever with a uniform load and an end load
Determine the internal bending moment $M_C$ at point $C$.
Solution
-30.6 kN*m
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagram of segment CB
Step 1Draw the free-body diagram: Cut the beam at $C$ and isolate the right segment $CB$. The forces on it are the distributed load on that piece, the end load $P = 6\ \mathrm{kN}$, and the exposed $N_C$, $V_C$ and $M_C$ on the cut face.
Step 2List knowns and unknown: $L = 4\ \mathrm{m}$, $w = 5\ \mathrm{kN/m}$, $P = 6\ \mathrm{kN}$, $C$ at $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Unknown: $M_C$.
Step 3Set axes and sign convention: Take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive; internal loads follow the class convention ($N$ positive in tension, $V$ positive clockwise, $M$ positive concave up).
Step 4Explain the choice of segment: Segment $CB$ contains the free end, so the fixed-support reactions at $A$ are not needed. On its left face, positive $V_C$ is upward and positive $M_C$ is clockwise.
Step 5Find the segment length: $$L_{CB} = 4\ \mathrm{m} - 1.5\ \mathrm{m} = 2.5\ \mathrm{m}$$
Step 6Find the distributed load on the segment: $$F_{CB} = (5\ \mathrm{kN/m})(2.5\ \mathrm{m}) = 12.5\ \mathrm{kN}$$ acting at the middle of the segment, $1.25\ \mathrm{m}$ from $C$.
Step 7Sum forces in x on segment CB: No load has a horizontal component, so $\sum F_x = 0$ gives $N_C = 0$.
Step 8Sum moments about the cut C: This eliminates $V_C$ and $N_C$. Arms: $1.25\ \mathrm{m}$ for $F_{CB}$ and $2.5\ \mathrm{m}$ for $P$, both clockwise about $C$: $$\sum M_C = 0:\quad -M_C - F_{CB}(1.25) - P(2.5) = 0$$
Step 9Substitute numbers: $$-M_C - (12.5\ \mathrm{kN})(1.25\ \mathrm{m}) - (6\ \mathrm{kN})(2.5\ \mathrm{m}) = 0$$
Step 10Rearrange for the moment: $$M_C = -(15.63 + 15.0)\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$$
Step 11Solve: $$M_C = -30.6\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$$
Step 12State the result: $M_C = -30.6\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$; the negative sign means the beam bends concave down at $C$.
Step 13Check for reasonableness: The moment at the wall is $-(5)(4)(2) - (6)(4) = -64\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$. The magnitude at $C$ is smaller, as it should be closer to the free end, and the units are $\mathrm{kN\cdot m}$.
Worked Solution
Free-body diagram of segment CB
**Given:** $L = 4\ \mathrm{m}$, $w = 5\ \mathrm{kN/m}$, $P = 6\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $B$, $C$ at $1.5\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$. **Find:** $M_C$. Keep the free-end segment $CB$ of length $4 - 1.5 = 2.5\ \mathrm{m}$. Load on it: $(5)(2.5) = 12.5\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $1.25\ \mathrm{m}$ from $C$, plus $P$ at $2.5\ \mathrm{m}$. $\sum M_C = 0:\ -M_C - 12.5(1.25) - 6(2.5) = 0 \Rightarrow M_C = -(15.63 + 15.0) = -30.6\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$. The negative sign means the beam bends concave down at $C$ (top fibers in tension), as expected for a cantilever.
Problem 6
Ch. 7 — Section Moment Under a Triangular Load
A simply supported beam $AB$ of length $6\ \mathrm{m}$ (pin at $A$, roller at $B$) supports stored material whose load varies linearly from zero at $A$ to $w_0 = 9\ \mathrm{kN/m}$ at $B$. Point $C$ is $4\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Neglect the weight of the beam. Use the sign convention from class: normal force $N$ is positive in tension, shear force $V$ is positive when it tends to rotate the segment clockwise, and bending moment $M$ is positive when it bends the segment concave up.
Simply supported beam with a triangular distributed load
Determine the internal bending moment $M_C$ at point $C$.
Solution
20.0 kN*m
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagrams of the whole beam and of segment AC
Step 1Draw the free-body diagrams: Isolate the whole beam. Forces: the triangular load (zero at $A$, $9\ \mathrm{kN/m}$ at $B$), pin reactions $A_x$, $A_y$, and roller reaction $B_y$.
Step 2List knowns and unknown: $L = 6\ \mathrm{m}$, $w_0 = 9\ \mathrm{kN/m}$ at $B$, $C$ at $4\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Unknown: $M_C$.
Step 3Set axes and sign convention: Take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive; internal loads follow the class convention ($N$ positive in tension, $V$ positive clockwise, $M$ positive concave up).
Step 4Find the resultant of the whole triangular load: Its magnitude is the triangle area, $$F_R = \tfrac{1}{2}(9\ \mathrm{kN/m})(6\ \mathrm{m}) = 27\ \mathrm{kN}$$
Step 5Locate the resultant: It acts at the centroid of the triangle, two thirds of the base from the zero end: $\tfrac{2}{3}(6) = 4\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$, which is $2\ \mathrm{m}$ from $B$.
Step 6Sum moments about B: Point $B$ removes $B_y$. Arms: $6\ \mathrm{m}$ for $A_y$, $2\ \mathrm{m}$ for $F_R$: $$\sum M_B = 0:\quad -A_y(6) + F_R(2) = 0$$
Step 7Substitute and solve for the pin reaction: $$A_y = \frac{(27\ \mathrm{kN})(2\ \mathrm{m})}{6\ \mathrm{m}} = 9.00\ \mathrm{kN}$$
Step 8Find the load intensity at C: By similar triangles, $$w_C = 9\left(\frac{4}{6}\right) = 6\ \mathrm{kN/m}$$
Step 9Cut the beam at C: Keep the left segment $AC$ ($4\ \mathrm{m}$). It carries $A_y$, a smaller triangular load rising from 0 to $6\ \mathrm{kN/m}$, and the exposed $N_C$, $V_C$ (downward) and $M_C$ (counterclockwise) on its right face.
Step 10Find the load on the segment: $$F_{AC} = \tfrac{1}{2}(6\ \mathrm{kN/m})(4\ \mathrm{m}) = 12\ \mathrm{kN}$$ acting one third of the segment length from its tall end: $\tfrac{1}{3}(4) = 1.333\ \mathrm{m}$ from $C$.
Step 11Sum moments about the cut C: This eliminates $N_C$ and $V_C$. Arms: $4\ \mathrm{m}$ for $A_y$ (clockwise), $1.333\ \mathrm{m}$ for $F_{AC}$ (counterclockwise): $$\sum M_C = 0:\quad M_C - A_y(4) + F_{AC}(1.333) = 0$$
Step 12Substitute numbers: $$M_C - (9.00\ \mathrm{kN})(4\ \mathrm{m}) + (12\ \mathrm{kN})(1.333\ \mathrm{m}) = 0$$
Step 13Solve for the moment: $$M_C = 36.0 - 16.0 = 20.0\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$$
Step 14State the result: $M_C = 20.0\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$, positive (concave up).
Step 15Check with the right segment: $B_y = 27 - 9 = 18\ \mathrm{kN}$. The load on $CB$ is a trapezoid, $(6)(2) = 12\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $1\ \mathrm{m}$ plus $\tfrac{1}{2}(3)(2) = 3\ \mathrm{kN}$ at $1.333\ \mathrm{m}$ from $C$, so $M_C = 18(2) - 12(1) - 3(1.333) = 20.0\ \mathrm{kN\cdot m}$, which agrees.

Problem 20
Ch. 7 — Shear at a Section of a Cantilever with a Triangular Load
A cantilever retaining post is modeled as a horizontal beam fixed to a wall at its left end $A$ and free at its right end $B$; its length is $3\ \mathrm{m}$. It carries a triangular distributed load that is largest at the wall, $w_0 = 6\ \mathrm{kN/m}$ at $A$, and decreases linearly to zero at the free end $B$. Point $C$ is $1\ \mathrm{m}$ from the wall. Neglect the weight of the beam. Use the sign convention from class: normal force $N$ is positive in tension, shear force $V$ is positive when it tends to rotate the segment clockwise, and bending moment $M$ is positive when it bends the segment concave up.
Cantilever with a triangular load largest at the wall
Determine the internal shear force $V_C$ at point $C$.
Solution
4.00 kN
Free-body diagram
Free-body diagram of segment CB
Step 1Draw the free-body diagram: Cut the beam at $C$ and isolate the right segment $CB$. The forces on it are the part of the triangular load between $C$ and $B$ and the exposed $N_C$, $V_C$ and $M_C$ on the cut face.
Step 2List knowns and unknown: Length $3\ \mathrm{m}$; $w_0 = 6\ \mathrm{kN/m}$ at $A$, zero at $B$; $C$ at $1\ \mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Unknown: $V_C$.
Step 3Set axes and sign convention: Take $+x$ to the right, $+y$ upward, and counterclockwise moments as positive; internal loads follow the class convention ($N$ positive in tension, $V$ positive clockwise, $M$ positive concave up).
Step 4Explain the choice of segment: Segment $CB$ contains the free end, so the wall reactions are not needed. On its left face, positive $V_C$ is drawn upward.
Step 5Find the segment length: $$L_{CB} = 3\ \mathrm{m} - 1\ \mathrm{m} = 2\ \mathrm{m}$$
Step 6Find the load intensity at C: The load is zero at $B$ and grows linearly toward $A$, so by similar triangles $$w_C = 6\left(\frac{2}{3}\right) = 4\ \mathrm{kN/m}$$
Step 7Find the load on the segment: The load on $CB$ is a triangle of height $4\ \mathrm{kN/m}$ and base $2\ \mathrm{m}$: $$F_{CB} = \tfrac{1}{2}(4\ \mathrm{kN/m})(2\ \mathrm{m}) = 4\ \mathrm{kN}$$
Step 8Locate the segment load: It acts at the centroid of the triangle, one third of the base from the tall end: $\tfrac{1}{3}(2) = 0.667\ \mathrm{m}$ from $C$. This arm is needed only for the moment, not for the shear.
Step 9Sum forces in x on segment CB: There are no horizontal loads, so $\sum F_x = 0$ gives $N_C = 0$.
Step 10Sum forces in y on segment CB: $$\sum F_y = 0:\quad V_C - F_{CB} = 0$$
Step 11Substitute numbers: $$V_C - 4\ \mathrm{kN} = 0$$
Step 12Solve for the shear: $$V_C = 4.00\ \mathrm{kN}$$
Step 13State the result: $V_C = 4.00\ \mathrm{kN}$, positive, so it acts in the assumed direction (upward on the left face of $CB$).
Step 14Check with the wall side: The total load is $\tfrac{1}{2}(6)(3) = 9\ \mathrm{kN}$, so $A_y = 9\ \mathrm{kN}$. The load between $A$ and $C$ is the trapezoid $\tfrac{1}{2}(6 + 4)(1) = 5\ \mathrm{kN}$, and $V_C = 9 - 5 = 4.00\ \mathrm{kN}$, which agrees.
